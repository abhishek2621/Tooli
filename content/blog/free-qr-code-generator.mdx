## What Is a QR Code?

A QR (Quick Response) code is a two-dimensional barcode capable of storing significantly more information than traditional barcodes.

Unlike regular barcodes that only contain numbers, QR codes can store:

- Website URLs
- Contact information
- Email addresses
- Phone numbers
- Google Maps locations
- Wi-Fi credentials
- Event details
- Product information

When someone scans the QR code using their smartphone camera, the stored information is displayed instantly.

For most businesses, the most common use is converting a website URL into a scannable QR code.

# Best Practices for Creating Effective QR Codes

Generating a QR code is easy.

Creating one that consistently scans is where best practices matter.

## Always Test Before Printing

Before printing hundreds—or even thousands—of copies, scan your QR code using multiple smartphones.

Check:

- Android
- iPhone
- Different camera apps
- Different lighting conditions

This simple step can save costly reprints.

---

## Use Secure HTTPS Links

Always link to secure websites whenever possible.

Users are more likely to trust destinations beginning with **https://** than unsecured websites.

---

## Leave Enough White Space

QR codes require a clear margin around them.

Removing this "quiet zone" can make scanning difficult.

Avoid placing text or graphics too close to the edges.

---

## Print at an Appropriate Size

Tiny QR codes can become difficult to scan.

As a general rule:

- Business cards → at least 0.8 inches
- Flyers → 1 inch or larger
- Posters → 2 inches or larger

If users need to scan from a distance, increase the size accordingly.

---

## Use High-Quality Images

Avoid blurry screenshots of QR codes.

Instead, download the generated QR code at full resolution before printing.

High-quality images ensure reliable scanning across all devices.

---

## Keep the Destination Relevant

Your QR code should take users exactly where they expect.

For example:

- Restaurant poster → Menu
- Product package → Product guide
- Business card → Company website

Unexpected destinations reduce trust and engagement.

# QR Codes vs Traditional Barcodes

People often confuse QR codes with barcodes, but they serve different purposes.

| QR Code                                     | Barcode                                |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Stores thousands of characters              | Stores only limited numbers            |
| Can contain URLs                            | Usually contains product IDs           |
| Read from any direction                     | Must be scanned horizontally           |
| Smartphone compatible                       | Requires barcode scanner in most cases |
| Supports websites, contacts, Wi-Fi and more | Mainly used for inventory              |

For digital marketing and online sharing, QR codes are significantly more versatile than traditional barcodes.

# Are QR Codes Safe?

One of the most common questions people ask is whether QR codes are safe.

The short answer is **yes**.

A QR code is simply a way to store information. By itself, it cannot install malware or damage your device.

However, just like clicking an unknown link, scanning a QR code from an untrusted source may lead to a malicious website.

## Tips for Staying Safe

- Only scan QR codes from trusted businesses.
- Check the destination URL before opening it.
- Look for secure websites using **https://**.
- Avoid entering personal information on suspicious websites.
- If something looks unusual, don't continue.

Modern Android and iPhone devices usually display the destination website before opening it, allowing you to verify the link first.

# Common QR Code Mistakes to Avoid

Even professionally designed QR codes sometimes fail because of simple mistakes.

## Printing the QR Code Too Small

Tiny QR codes are difficult to scan.

Always test your printed copy before mass production.

---

## Using Low-Resolution Images

A blurry QR code may become unreadable.

Always download and print the highest-quality version available.

---

## Linking to the Wrong Page

Double-check your URL before generating the QR code.

A single typing mistake can send visitors to an error page.

---

## Poor Color Contrast

Dark QR codes on light backgrounds provide the best scanning performance.

Avoid:

- Yellow on white
- Light gray on white
- Transparent backgrounds
- Busy background images

---

## Not Testing the QR Code

Always scan the code using multiple devices before printing brochures, business cards, banners, or packaging.

Testing only takes a minute but can prevent expensive printing mistakes.
